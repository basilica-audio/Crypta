# Factory impulse responses — provenance and licences

**Status: four impulse responses are bundled with Crypta, and every one of them
was generated by this repository rather than recorded by anyone.** That is the
whole licensing argument, and it is deliberately the simplest one available.

> **These are models, not captures.** Nothing in this folder is a recording of
> any cabinet, speaker or microphone. Each file is computed by
> [`tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py`](../../tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py) from a
> published analytical model — a cascade of standard analog-prototype filter
> sections plus reflection taps derived from physical path lengths. They are
> voiced from the documented behaviour of moving-coil loudspeakers in boxes, and
> their display names all begin with **"Modelled"** so that a user reading a
> preset list is never left thinking otherwise.

## Why generated rather than sourced

Bundling a cabinet IR means redistributing a recording inside every copy of an
AGPLv3 binary. A capture carries rights from the cabinet, from the microphone,
and from whoever pressed record; third-party packs routinely misstate all three,
and "free download" is not a licence. Sourcing one that clears that bar is a
research project with an uncertain answer.

A generated IR has no such question. There is no third-party recording in it, so
there is nothing to trace, nothing to dispute, and no licensor to find. The
generator is committed alongside the audio: run it, get byte-identical output,
compare the SHA-256 below. **Reproducibility is the provenance.**

## The bar (enforced in code)

Crypta accepts only three licence categories, and
[`src/dsp/FactoryIRs.cpp`](../../src/dsp/FactoryIRs.cpp) drops any asset that
does not name one *and* a checkable source:

| Licence string | Meaning | What must be recorded |
|---|---|---|
| `CC0-1.0` | Creative Commons Zero — dedicated to the public domain | Where it came from, and the date |
| `Public Domain` | An explicit public-domain dedication that is not CC0 | The wording of the dedication and where it is published |
| `Self-recorded` | Captured by this project on gear we own | Cab, speaker, mic, position, date, engineer |

Explicitly **not** accepted:

- "free download", "free for commercial use", "royalty free" — not licences, and
  none of them grants redistribution inside a binary.
- CC-BY / CC-BY-SA — usable, but they carry attribution (and, for SA,
  share-alike) obligations into every downstream copy. Taking that on is a
  deliberate decision for the project owner, not a default.
- Anything whose origin cannot be traced to a named licensor. "It was on a forum"
  is not provenance.

The bundled set is filed under **`CC0-1.0`**, not `Self-recorded`, because
`Self-recorded` means a *capture* and these are not captures — calling them one
would be the exact mislabelling this file exists to prevent. Yves Vogl, as the
author of the generator and therefore of its output, dedicates these four files
to the public domain under CC0 1.0 Universal; the full legal code is committed
next to them in [`CC0-1.0.txt`](CC0-1.0.txt), retrieved from
<https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.txt>. The `source`
field of each asset in
[`CryptaAudioProcessor::getFactoryIRAssetTable()`](../../src/PluginProcessor.cpp)
names the generator and the model id, both of which a reviewer can check without
asking anyone.

## Attribution

None is required — CC0 waives it. Nothing in this folder obliges a downstream
user of a Crypta binary to credit anybody, which is the point.

## Bundled impulse responses

All four are mono, 24-bit PCM, 48 kHz, normalised so that the maximum of the
magnitude response is exactly 0 dBFS (so no sine at any frequency can leave the
convolver hotter than it entered it).

### Modelled 8x10 Cone

- **File** `modelled_8x10_cone.wav`, mono, 24-bit PCM, 48 kHz, 4096 samples (85.3 ms)
- **Models** a sealed 8x10 bass stack with a generic cardioid dynamic on the dust
  cap at 2 cm: an under-damped second-order box alignment at 55 Hz, the 10" cone's
  upper-mid growl at 700 Hz and 1.5 kHz, its breakup null at 2.6 kHz, a 24 dB/oct
  inductance roll-off from 4 kHz, plus baffle-edge and floor reflections at 26 cm
  and 55 cm of excess path.
- **Generated by** `tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py`, model id `bass-810-cone`
- **Licence** CC0-1.0 (dedicated by Yves Vogl, Basilica Audio, 2026-08-22)
- **SHA-256** `67aa93d0e238e88b74b455c3b5322d455d61c007620ace2d2cb88b54c6a793e2`

### Modelled 8x10 Edge

- **File** `modelled_8x10_edge.wav`, mono, 24-bit PCM, 48 kHz, 4096 samples (85.3 ms)

  for the cone's directivity, and +2 dB at 220 Hz for the low-mid the edge

- **Generated by** `tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py`, model id `bass-810-edge`

### Modelled 4x10 Horn

- **File** `modelled_4x10_horn.wav`, mono, 24-bit PCM, 48 kHz, 4096 samples (85.3 ms)
- **Models** a ported 4x10 with an HF horn on a passive crossover — the modern,
  extended-top bass voicing. Two parallel paths: a woofer path low-passed at
  24 dB/oct from 3 kHz, and a horn path high-passed at 24 dB/oct from 3.5 kHz,
  at 0.5 gain and delayed by 4 samples because a horn's acoustic centre sits
  behind the woofer baffle plane. That offset is what makes the crossover region
  comb the way a real one does.
- **Generated by** `tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py`, model id `bass-410-horn`
- **Licence** CC0-1.0 (dedicated by Yves Vogl, Basilica Audio, 2026-08-22)
- **SHA-256** `b7e98317d45a340cff6853392a1fde3bcf4f446a6a7e5f7a0336dabeca1537f4`

## Verified measurements

Measured by `tools/ir-synth/verify_irs.py` from the shipped files, not from the
code that wrote them. Corners are read off a 1/3-octave RMS-smoothed magnitude
response, because a cabinet response is a comb of narrow breakup peaks and
walking outwards from a raw-spectrum maximum measures the skirt of one peak
rather than the cabinet's bandwidth.

| File | peak dBFS | RMS dBFS | max\|H(f)\| | DC rel. peak | −10 dB band | RT (T20) | last sample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `modelled_8x10_cone.wav` | −18.38 | −47.86 | 0.000 dB | −113.9 dB | 32 Hz – 4699 Hz | 33.3 ms | 0 |
| `modelled_8x10_edge.wav` | −22.95 | −50.04 | −0.000 dB | −108.1 dB | 34 Hz – 2172 Hz | 34.8 ms | 0 |
| `modelled_1x15_vintage.wav` | −26.47 | −53.28 | −0.000 dB | −69.0 dB | 34 Hz – 1804 Hz | 63.0 ms | 0 |
| `modelled_4x10_horn.wav` | −20.05 | −48.54 | 0.000 dB | −76.0 dB | 37 Hz – 3079 Hz | 49.4 ms | 0 |

No file contains a clipped sample, a non-finite sample, or a DC offset above
1e-4. `tests/FactoryIRTests.cpp` re-measures every bundled asset through the
plugin's own decoder and convolution path, so a regression fails the build
rather than the release.

## How to add one

1. If it is generated: add the model to `tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py`, re-run it
   into this folder, and re-run `verify_irs.py`.
2. If it is a third-party asset: verify the licence at the source and archive the
   evidence (the licence page itself, not a third party's description of it).
3. Add the WAV to `juce_add_binary_data()` in `CMakeLists.txt`.
4. Add the entry to `CryptaAudioProcessor::getFactoryIRAssetTable()` in
   `src/PluginProcessor.cpp`, with its licence string and a source field a
   reviewer can check without asking anyone.
5. Add a section to this file.
6. `tests/FactoryIRTests.cpp` fails if the entry does not clear the bar, so the
   build is the last line of defence rather than the review.
